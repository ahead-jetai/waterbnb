# Los Angeles pilot launch checklist

**Operator:** Arthur Head. **Support:** ahead747@gmail.com. **Scope:** invite-only, moored overnight stays in Los Angeles, California. The guest may not operate or move the boat. The booking does not include a charter, a voyage, or transportation.

The database defaults to **bookings disabled** and publishes no existing or new listing automatically. The app can be deployed for review without admitting paid stays. These are launch criteria, not a legal determination that an individual berth may be rented.

## 1. Clear the berth, business, and policy requirements

- Identify the exact marina, slip, and governing jurisdiction. Keep written marina or harbor approval and confirm the slip agreement permits paid guest overnights. An owner’s permission or proof of boat ownership does not establish permission to rent a slip. Verify applicable planning, lodging, licensing, and tax obligations with the authority for that berth. For locations in the City of Los Angeles, consult [Los Angeles City Planning's Home-Sharing program](https://planning.lacity.gov/project-review/home-sharing) to ask which rules apply; do not assume its residential registration program covers vessels. For a County-managed site, consult [Los Angeles County Beaches and Harbors](https://beaches.lacounty.gov/) and the actual marina operator. Do not approve a listing until the applicable rules are confirmed.
- Confirm insurance in writing with a broker or carrier for the host's and platform's roles. Verify commercial paid overnight use, guest boarding and dock access, the berth and vessel, liability, exclusions, and expiry. WaterBnB does not advertise included insurance, earnings, or 24/7 support.
- Have the operator and qualified counsel review `/terms`, `/privacy`, and `/cancellation`. Confirm the operator's required contact disclosures, privacy rights and retention, and any disclosures, taxes, consent, or acknowledgement required at sign-up and Checkout. Remove the Terms and Privacy draft labels only after sign-off and record the reviewed version.
- Send a test email from outside the operator's account. Confirm the invite, refund, privacy, and safety topics reach the inbox and can be handled. Check a reply reaches the sender. Escalate immediate danger to local emergency services, not email. Write down who handles refunds, temporary pauses and incident follow-up before inviting real guests.

## 2. Vet each host and listing

Keep evidence in an access-restricted operator record, not in this repository or in a public listing. Record the reviewer and date, verified host and right to offer the vessel, berth approval and permit findings, coverage and expiry, and the safety walkthrough.

- Check the allowed capacity and current photos, safe dock/boarding access and mobility limitations, emergency access and exits, lifejackets sized for guests, fire and carbon monoxide precautions, availability of sanitation, emergency contacts, and a plan for unsafe tides, weather, and closures. Ask the marina what is required for that vessel and location. Reference [California Division of Boating and Waterways](https://dbw.parks.ca.gov/?page_id=28711) and the [US Coast Guard Recreational Boating Safety site](https://www.uscgboating.org/); guest operation and passenger transport are outside this pilot.
- Give guests clear check-in and access instructions and verify the schedule and exact berth locally. Check that Stripe Connect onboarding is complete and that the host can receive transfers in the intended environment.
- Start with a small group of vetted hosts and invited guests. Check each stay and respond to issues before expanding. Material changes to listing content withdraw publication and require another review; price and booking-mode changes do not. Revoking a host's invitation unpublishes their listings.

## 3. Rehearse before activation

1. Complete the [staging runbook](staging-runbook.md), including both accounts, Stripe sandbox, hosted backups and a restore. Apply the pilot migration and deploy the frontend and functions together in staging. Do not enable live payments to test this checklist.
2. Set [Clerk Access Mode to Invite-only](https://clerk.com/docs/guides/secure/restricting-access#invite-only) on the intended instance. Verify uninvited sign-up is denied and the invitation link works with the configured sign-in factors. Record the resulting Clerk user IDs. Clerk controls account admission; database pilot invitations independently control booking eligibility.
3. In the staging database, have an operator add `pilot_invitations` rows with the verified Clerk ID and role (`host` or `guest`). Set `listing_check_in_schedules` for each vetted listing with the actual local check-in time and `America/Los_Angeles`. After the review, set `listings.pilot_approved_at` for the exact verified listing ID and host. Do not bulk-approve old inventory or approve fixtures.
4. Confirm the draft and invite records are not publicly writable. Check an empty catalog, an approved stay, a failed catalog response, and a private listing URL. Follow the footer links as a signed-out user and confirm support messages contain only the intended reference, not guest data. Open the staging pilot only for the approved test accounts by setting the singleton `pilot_settings.bookings_enabled` to `true`. Verify a second signed-in account without a guest invitation cannot request or pay.
5. Keep the production switch `false` until the above evidence and the operator's release approval are recorded. Opening it admits only eligible bookings, not public access. For a pause, set the switch back to `false`. This prevents new and resumed Checkout links from the app; **also expire open links already issued in Stripe** and reconcile pending payments. Do not block valid webhooks, existing trips, or refunds.
